\begin{abstract}
Let \(3\text{-}\mathrm{QI}(n,2)\) be the \(3\)-uniform hypergraph whose
vertices are binary bipartitions of an \(n\)-set with both classes of size at
least \(4\), and whose edges are triples realizing all eight binary patterns.
We prove that \(3\text{-}\mathrm{QI}(n,2)\) is a core for every \(n\ge8\).
The proof has two parts.  First, we prove that every uniform middle layer
\(3\text{-}\mathrm{UQI}(n,2)\) is a core for even \(n\ge8\).  The cases
\(n\equiv2\pmod4\) are reduced to the nonexistence of a Steiner system,
while the cases \(n\equiv0\pmod4\) are handled by a two-anchor
critical-fold obstruction and a Sperner bound on link cliques.  Second, we
prove a middle-layer rigidity theorem: if the most balanced layer is a core,
then the full qualitative-independence hypergraph is a core.  This uses an
exact crossing-family bound, endomorphism invariance of the middle layer, a
finite-order power argument, and a balanced-anchor separation construction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A finite hypergraph is a \emph{core} if every endomorphism is an
automorphism.  Cores are the minimal representatives of homomorphic
equivalence classes and retain homomorphism-monotone invariants.  Qualitative
independence hypergraphs encode covering arrays: a triple of binary columns
is adjacent precisely when all eight binary row patterns occur.

Thomas and Akhtar initiated the systematic study of cores of binary
\(3\)-qualitative independence hypergraphs, proved the case \(n=8\), and
left the determination of the core of \(3\text{-}\mathrm{QI}(n,2)\) for
\(n>8\) as an open problem \cite{ThomasAkhtar2026}.  They also related the
uniform and almost-uniform middle layers to merged Johnson graphs.

For odd \(n\ge9\), the core property of the almost-uniform middle layer was
proved in \cite{KimMergedJohnson2026} by determining the endomorphism monoid
of the relevant merged Johnson graph.  The present paper supplies the even
middle layers and then lifts the middle-layer result to the full hypergraph.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every integer \(n\ge8\), the hypergraph
\[
3\text{-}\mathrm{QI}(n,2)
\]
is a core.
\end{theorem}

Two intermediate results may be useful beyond the present application.

\begin{theorem}[Even middle layers]\label{thm:even-middle}
For every even integer \(n\ge8\),
\[
3\text{-}\mathrm{UQI}(n,2)
\]
is a core.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Middle-layer lift]\label{thm:lift}
Let \(B_n\) be the most balanced layer of
\(H_n=3\text{-}\mathrm{QI}(n,2)\).  If \(B_n\) is a core, then \(H_n\) is a
core.
\end{theorem}

\section{Qualitative independence and cores}

Let \(\Omega\) be an \(n\)-element set.  A binary string is identified,
up to interchanging its two symbols, with an unordered bipartition
\[
[A]=\{A,\Omega\setminus A\}.
\]
The vertices of \(H_n=3\text{-}\mathrm{QI}(n,2)\) are those bipartitions
whose two blocks have size at least \(4\).  Three vertices
\([A],[B],[C]\) form an edge when all eight cells
\[
A^{\epsilon_1}\cap B^{\epsilon_2}\cap C^{\epsilon_3},
\qquad
(\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3)\in\{0,1\}^3,
\]
are nonempty.  This condition is independent of the chosen orientations.

The \emph{middle layer} \(B_n\) consists of the bipartitions with block
sizes
\[
\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor,
\qquad
\left\lceil\frac n2\right\rceil.
\]
It is \(3\text{-}\mathrm{UQI}(n,2)\) for even \(n\) and
\(3\text{-}\mathrm{AUQI}(n,2)\) for odd \(n\).

The \(2\)-section \([H]_2\) of a \(3\)-uniform hypergraph \(H\) is the
graph on \(V(H)\) in which two vertices are adjacent when they lie together
in a hyperedge.

\begin{lemma}[Graph-to-hypergraph transfer]\label{lem:transfer}
If \([H]_2\) is a core, then \(H\) is a core.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every endomorphism of \(H\) is an endomorphism of \([H]_2\).  If
\([H]_2\) is a core, that map is injective.  On a finite vertex set it is
bijective.  The induced map on \(3\)-subsets is then a permutation, and it
maps the finite edge set injectively into itself, hence onto itself.
\end{proof}

\section{Crossing cuts and the middle-layer lift}

For a \(t\)-set \(T\), let \(\mathrm{Cross}(t)\) be the graph whose vertices
are nontrivial unordered bipartitions of \(T\), two of which are adjacent
when all four cells of their common refinement are nonempty.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:cross}
For \(t\ge4\),
\[
\omega(\mathrm{Cross}(t))
=q(t):=\binom{t-1}{\lfloor t/2\rfloor-1}.
\]
Moreover, \(q(t+1)>q(t)\) for every \(t\ge4\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If \(t=2r\), orient each cut toward a fixed point \(p\).  Removing \(p\)
from the chosen sides gives an antichain on \(2r-1\) points, so Sperner's
theorem gives the upper bound \(\binom{2r-1}{r-1}\).  Equality is attained
by all \(r\)-subsets containing \(p\).

If \(t=2r+1\), the complements of the oriented traces form an intersecting
antichain on \(2r\) points.  Milner's intersecting-antichain theorem gives
\(\binom{2r}{r-1}\), attained by all \(r\)-subsets containing \(p\)
\cite{Milner1968}.  The ratios
\[
\frac{q(2r+1)}{q(2r)}=\frac{2r}{r+1}>1,
\qquad
\frac{q(2r+2)}{q(2r+1)}=\frac{2r+1}{r}>1
\]
give strict monotonicity.
\end{proof}

For \(T\in V(H_n)\), let \(L_T\) denote the link graph:
\[
R\sim_{L_T}S
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\{T,R,S\}\in E(H_n).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Intrinsic middle layer]\label{lem:middle-invariant}
Every endomorphism of \(H_n\) maps \(B_n\) into \(B_n\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let the smaller block of \(T\) have size \(t\).  Restriction to that block
maps every link clique injectively into \(\mathrm{Cross}(t)\), hence
\[
\omega(L_T)\le q(t).
\]
If \(T\in B_n\), let \(b=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\).  Take a maximum
\(q(b)\)-crossing family on the \(b\)-block.  On the other block take a
\(q(b)\)-element crossing family: when \(n\) is odd, this is a subfamily of
a maximum \(q(b+1)\)-family.  Pair the local cuts and join corresponding
sides.  The resulting global bipartitions form a \(q(b)\)-clique in
\(L_T\).  Their two blocks have size at least \(4\), including the boundary
cases \(n=8,9\).

An endomorphism maps a link clique of size at least two injectively into a
link clique.  Thus, for \(T\in B_n\),
\[
q(b)=\omega(L_T)\le\omega(L_{f(T)})\le q(t(f(T))).
\]
Strict monotonicity and \(t(f(T))\le b\) imply \(t(f(T))=b\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Balanced-anchor separation]\label{lem:separation}
For distinct \(X,Y\in V(H_n)\), there exist \(R,S\in B_n\) such that
\[
\{X,R,S\}\in E(H_n),
\qquad
\{Y,R,S\}\notin E(H_n).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(X=\{X_0,X_1\}\) and \(Y=\{Y_0,Y_1\}\).  Since \(X\ne Y\), some
block \(E\) of \(Y\) meets both blocks of \(X\); otherwise the two
partitions coincide.  Put \(D=E^c\).

Colour \(\Omega\) with the four colours \(00,01,10,11\).  Choose one point
in each \(X_i\cap E\) and colour those two points \(11\).  In each \(X_i\),
colour three additional points \(00,01,10\).  Every \(X_i\) now contains
all four colours, while \(D\) contains no point of colour \(11\).

For \(n=2k\), complete the colour multiplicities to
\[
(c_{00},c_{01},c_{10},c_{11})=(2,k-2,k-2,2);
\]
for \(n=2k+1\), complete them to
\[
(3,k-2,k-2,2).
\]
The remaining required multiplicities are nonnegative because \(k\ge4\).
Define
\[
R=\{x:c(x)\in\{10,11\}\},
\qquad
S=\{x:c(x)\in\{01,11\}\}.
\]
Then \(R,S\in B_n\), every \(X\)-block sees all four joint colours, and
\(D\cap R\cap S=\varnothing\).  This gives the two claimed incidences.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:lift}]
Let \(f\in\End(H_n)\).  By Lemma~\ref{lem:middle-invariant},
\(g=f|_{B_n}\) is an endomorphism of \(B_n\), hence an automorphism.  Let
\(d\) be its finite order and set \(h=f^d\).  Then \(h\) fixes \(B_n\)
pointwise.

If \(h(X)=Y\ne X\), choose \(R,S\in B_n\) from
Lemma~\ref{lem:separation}.  Applying \(h\) maps the edge \(\{X,R,S\}\) to the
nonedge \(\{Y,R,S\}\), a contradiction.  Hence \(f^d=h=\mathrm{id}\), so
\(f\) is bijective and its inverse is the endomorphism \(f^{d-1}\).
\end{proof}

\section{Even middle layers: the two modulo four branch}

Let \(n=2k\), where \(k\ge5\) is odd, and let
\[
H_k=3\text{-}\mathrm{UQI}(2k,2),
\qquad
G_k=[H_k]_2.
\]
The vertices are unordered equipartitions \([A]=\{A,A^c\}\), with
\(|A|=k\).  If \(s=|A\cap B|\), the four pair cells have sizes
\(s,k-s,k-s,s\).  A balanced third part splits all four cells if and only if
\[
2\le s\le k-2.
\]
For sufficiency, choose its intersections with the four cells to have sizes
\[
1,\quad 1,\quad k-s-1,\quad s-1.
\]

Fix a point in the \(2k\)-set.  The quotient vertices become the
\(r\)-subsets of a \(v\)-set, where
\[
r=k-1,\qquad v=2r+1=2k-1.
\]
Let \(\Gamma\) join two \(r\)-subsets when their intersection has size
\(0\) or \(r-1\).  Then
\[
G_k=\overline{\Gamma}.
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:gamma-clique}
\(\Gamma\) is vertex-transitive and \(\omega(\Gamma)=r+2\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fixing an \((r-1)\)-subset gives a clique of size \(r+2\).  For the upper
bound, first suppose that a clique contains disjoint \(A,B\).  There is one
point \(x\) outside \(A\cup B\).  If \(C\) is adjacent to both, then
\[
|C\cap A|+|C\cap B|+\boldsymbol{1}_{x\in C}=r,
\]
with both intersections in \(\{0,r-1\}\).  Hence \(C\) is
\[
(A\setminus\{a\})\cup\{x\}
\quad\text{or}\quad
(B\setminus\{b\})\cup\{x\}.
\]
One set of each type meets in the singleton \(\{x\}\), so the two types
cannot be mixed when \(r\ge3\).  The clique has at most \(2+r=r+2\)
members.

Now suppose that there is no disjoint pair.  All pairwise intersections
have size \(r-1\).  Write
\[
A=D\cup\{a\},\qquad B=D\cup\{b\},\qquad |D|=r-1.
\]
Every further member either contains \(D\), or is an \(r\)-subset of
\(D\cup\{a,b\}\).  If only the first type occurs, the family is contained
in the \(r+2\) sets obtained by adjoining one point to \(D\).  If the
second type occurs, no set \(D\cup\{z\}\) with
\(z\notin D\cup\{a,b\}\) is compatible with it, and the clique is contained
in the \(r+1\) \(r\)-subsets of \(D\cup\{a,b\}\).  This proves the bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Design implication]\label{lem:design-implication}
If
\[
\chi(\overline\Gamma)=\omega(\overline\Gamma),
\]
then a Steiner system \(S(r-1,r,2r+1)\) exists.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a clique \(\mathcal Q\) and independent set \(\mathcal I\) of the
vertex-transitive graph \(\Gamma\), orbit averaging gives
\[
|\mathcal Q||\mathcal I|\le\binom{2r+1}{r}.
\]
Assume \(\chi(\overline\Gamma)=\omega(\overline\Gamma)=c\).  Since
\(\omega(\overline\Gamma)=\alpha(\Gamma)\), a \(c\)-colouring of
\(\overline\Gamma\) and Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma-clique} force
\[
c=\frac{\binom{2r+1}{r}}{r+2}
\]
and every colour class has size \(r+2\).

Take a maximum clique \(\mathcal B\) of \(\overline\Gamma\).  It is an
independent set in \(\Gamma\), so no two of its \(r\)-subsets share an
\((r-1)\)-subset.  Moreover,
\[
|\mathcal B|\,r
=\frac{\binom{2r+1}{r}}{r+2}\,r
=\binom{2r+1}{r-1}.
\]
Thus every \((r-1)\)-subset lies in exactly one member of \(\mathcal B\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:mod2}
If \(k\ge5\) is odd, then \(3\text{-}\mathrm{UQI}(2k,2)\) is a core.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The natural symmetric group is transitive on nonedges of \(G_k\).
Cameron and Kazanidis therefore imply that \(G_k\) is a core or has a
complete core \cite[Corollary 2.2]{CameronKazanidis2008}.  A finite graph
has a complete core exactly when its clique and chromatic numbers agree.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:design-implication}, equality would yield
\[
S(k-2,k-1,2k-1).
\]
In such a design the number of blocks through a fixed \((k-3)\)-subset is
\[
\lambda_{k-3}
=\frac{\binom{k+2}{1}}{\binom21}
=\frac{k+2}{2},
\]
which is not an integer for odd \(k\).  Hence the complete-core alternative
is impossible, so \(G_k\) is a core.  Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer}.
\end{proof}

\section{Even middle layers: the zero modulo four branch}

Let \(n=4m\) and use the fixed-point model
\[
V=\binom{\Omega}{2m-1},
\qquad |\Omega|=4m-1.
\]
Three vertices form an edge when the seven finite Boolean cells other than
\(111\) are nonempty.  The \(2\)-section joins \(X,Y\) when
\[
1\le |X\cap Y|\le2m-3.
\]
Thus distinct nonedges have intersection \(0\) or \(2m-2\), and form one
orbit under the full \(S_{4m}\)-action on equipartitions.

\begin{lemma}[Critical-fold criterion]\label{lem:critical-fold}
Suppose every link of a vertex-transitive \(3\)-uniform hypergraph has
clique number at most \(M\), its distinct \(2\)-section nonedges form one
orbit, and a standard nonedge \(P,Q\) has \(M+1\) auxiliary vertices such
that every auxiliary pair is in an edge with \(P\) or \(Q\).  Then the
hypergraph is a core.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If an endomorphism identifies \(P,Q\), the auxiliary images are distinct
and form an \((M+1)\)-clique in the link of the common image.  Every
possible collision is equivalent to this one.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:4m}
For every \(m\ge4\), \(3\text{-}\mathrm{UQI}(4m,2)\) is a core.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a link clique \(\mathcal C\) at \(T\), the traces
\(\{T\cap R:R\in\mathcal C\}\) form an antichain in \(2^T\).  Hence
\[
\omega(L_T)\le M_m:=\binom{2m-1}{m-1}.
\]

Choose disjoint \(K,\{p,q\},W\) with
\[
|K|=2m-2,\qquad |W|=2m-1,
\]
and put \(P=K\cup\{p\}\), \(Q=K\cup\{q\}\).  Fix
\(x\in K\) and distinct \(u,v\in W\), and put \(O=\{p\}\cup W\).

Partition \(A\in\binom{Q}{m-1}\) into the three types
\[
x\notin A,\qquad
x\in A,\ q\notin A,\qquad
x,q\in A.
\]
Match them bijectively with complement pairs of \(m\)-subsets of \(O\)
of the following respective types:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{type}&\text{condition}&\text{number}\\ \hline
0&u,v\text{ separated}&\binom{2m-2}{m-1}\\
10&\text{\(p\)-free representative contains }u,v&
\binom{2m-3}{m-2}\\
11&\text{\(p\)-free representative avoids }u,v&
\binom{2m-3}{m-3}.
\end{array}
\]
The three classes on the \(A\)-side have the same respective cardinalities:
choose \(m-1\) points while avoiding \(x\), choose \(x\) and \(m-2\)
further points while avoiding \(q\), or choose \(x,q\) and \(m-3\)
further points.  Thus the typewise bijections exist and together exhaust
both sides.

Let \(B_A\) be the chosen representative and set \(R_A=A\cup B_A\).
For distinct \(A,A'\), put
\[
s=|A\cap A'|,\qquad t=|B_A\cap B_{A'}|.
\]
Distinct equal-sized \(A,A'\) give \(0\le s\le m-2\).  Distinct assigned
complement pairs make \(B_A,B_{A'}\) neither equal nor complementary, so
\(1\le t\le m-1\).  In bit order \((Q,R_A,R_{A'})\), the seven required
atom sizes are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
 &000&001&010&011&100&101&110\\ \hline
\text{size}
 &t&m-t&m-t&t&s+1&m-1-s&m-1-s.
\end{array}
\]
They are all positive.  Hence distinct central vertices satisfy
\[
\{Q,R_A,R_{A'}\}\in E.
\]

Set
\[
E=\{x,p\}\cup(W\setminus\{u,v\}).
\]
The required exceptional atom sizes, in bit order
\((\text{anchor},E,R_A)\), are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|ccccccc}
\text{type}&\text{anchor}&000&001&010&011&100&101&110\\ \hline
0&Q&1&1&m-1&m-1&m-1&m-1&1\\
10&P&1&2&m-1&m-2&m-1&m-2&1\\
11&P&2&1&m-3&m&m&m-3&1.
\end{array}
\]
All are positive for \(m\ge4\).  Thus
\[
\{E\}\cup\left\{R_A:A\in\binom{Q}{m-1}\right\}
\]
has \(M_m+1\) vertices and satisfies Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-fold}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The exceptional value n=12}

The preceding construction has two zero cells at \(m=3\).  We use a
separate \(11\)-vertex obstruction.  In the model
\(V=\binom{[11]}5\), set
\[
P=\{0,1,2,3,4\},
\qquad
Q=\{0,1,2,3,5\}.
\]
The eleven auxiliary vertices and their anchor table are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:n12}.  Every link clique has size at most
\(\binom{5}{2}=10\), while identifying \(P,Q\) forces an \(11\)-clique in one
link.  Hence \(3\text{-}\mathrm{UQI}(12,2)\) is a core.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:even-middle}]
The case \(n=8\) is due to Thomas and Akhtar
\cite[Theorem 4]{ThomasAkhtar2026}.  Proposition \ref{prop:mod2} treats
\(n\equiv2\pmod4\), the preceding subsection treats \(n=12\), and
Proposition~\ref{prop:4m} treats \(n\equiv0\pmod4\), \(n\ge16\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
For even \(n\), the middle layer is a core by
Theorem~\ref{thm:even-middle}.  For odd \(n\ge9\), it is a core by
\cite{KimMergedJohnson2026}.  Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:lift}.  The case \(n=8\) is
already included in the even statement.
\end{proof}
